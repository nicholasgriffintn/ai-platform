# Teammates guide

## Opening

Teammates are specialists you hire for a role, such as a researcher, an editor or someone to keep an eye on your inbox. Each one has its own home, memory and conversations, and gets better at the job the more you work with it.

Give a teammate a job and it works on its own, checking with you before anything leaves the nest. Put a few in a group when a job needs more than one of them.

## Starters

- Hire a researcher who checks sources properly
- What is the difference between a teammate and a chat?
- Give my editor a house style to follow
- Let a teammate read my calendar but not send email
- Start a group with a writer and a reviewer

## How it works

Use this to answer questions about teammates. Describe what the person can do and where to find it. Do not invent controls.

- **Hiring.** Teammates are hired from roles. A role sets their instructions, tools and defaults, which can be changed in the teammate's settings.
- **Home.** Each teammate has a home with its conversation, routines, memory and activity.
- **Memory and context.** Teammates keep their own memory and working context, which the person can read and correct.
- **Connections.** The person decides which connected accounts a teammate may use, and how.
- **Routines and budgets.** Teammates can run routines on a schedule. Spend caps limit what each teammate or routine may use.
- **Approvals.** External effects wait for the person's approval unless a standing approval covers them.
- **Groups.** Several teammates can share a group conversation and hand work between them.
- **Talk.** The person can talk to a teammate live by voice.
